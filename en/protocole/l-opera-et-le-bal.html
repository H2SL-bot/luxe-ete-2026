The opera and the ball

Nowhere is protocol more explicit than in Vienna: the balls publish their dress code to the word, and the opera houses their regulations. What follows is not style advice; it is the rulebook.

What the institutions publish

  • Wiener Staatsoper (Wiener Opernball). At the Vienna Opera Ball, the dress code requires gentlemen to wear a black tailcoat with a white shirt, white bow tie and black patent shoes, gala uniform also being admitted as an option.
  • Wiener Staatsoper (Wiener Opernball). Ladies must wear a floor-length ball gown, in any colour, traditional costume and the festive dirndl being expressly excluded.
  • Wiener Staatsoper (Wiener Opernball). The evening follows a fixed order: entry at 20:15, the opening at 22:00, the midnight quadrille at 0:00, quadrilles at 2:00 and 4:00, and the end of the ball at 5:00 in the morning.
  • Wiener Philharmoniker. At the Wiener Philharmoniker Ball at the Musikverein, a long evening gown and tailcoat are required, with doors opening at 21:00 for a start at 22:00 and an end at 5:00.
  • Opéra national de Paris. At the Opéra de Paris, the auditorium doors are closed once the performance has begun and latecomers can be seated only at the interval, where the running of the performance allows for it, with no exchange or refund of the ticket.
  • Teatro alla Scala. La Scala refuses entry to the auditorium to spectators in shorts or sleeveless tops, and the ticket is not refunded in that case.

Each rule is taken from the document published by the institution, consulted at its official address on 27/08/2026.

Custom, according to the house

One arrives before the doors close: the opera house knows nothing of the fashionably late. One applauds neither between movements nor during a conductor's silence. At a ball, one does not cross the floor during a quadrille, and the opening curtsey belongs to the debutantes: one watches it, one does not mimic it.
