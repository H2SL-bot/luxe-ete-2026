The Protocol

Knowing how to get in is not enough. The radar tells you where to go and how to enter; the Wardrobe tells you what to wear; the Protocol tells you how to conduct yourself.

Every world has its rules, and the great institutions publish them: dress codes precise to the centimetre, immutable timetables, telephones banned. The Protocol gathers them, verified at the source, then adds custom: what is written nowhere yet noticed everywhere.

The golden rule

Only two things are published here: the rules written by the institutions themselves, consulted at the source, and the custom the house stands behind as its own counsel. Never folklore, never an invented rule.

  • The gala
    Black tie, white tie, replying to the invitation, timings: the protocol of galas and charity dinners, verified against the sources.
  • The auction
    Registering, raising the paddle, never cancelling a bid: auction protocol according to Christie's, Sotheby's and Phillips.
  • The opera and the ball
    Tailcoat compulsory, gown to the floor, latecomers seated at the interval: the protocol of the opera house and the great Viennese balls.
  • The races
    Hats compulsory, hemlines below the knee, telephones outside the enclosure: the protocol of Royal Ascot, Henley and ParisLongchamp.
  • The private members' club
    No photographs, no calls, no tipping: the published rules of private members' clubs, from Mayfair to New York.
  • The vernissage and the fair
    Invitation-only preview, photographs without flash, bags carried by hand: the protocol of art fairs and vernissages.
